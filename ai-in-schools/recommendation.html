Build your recommendation.

Make a clear choice. Show the evidence. Name the tradeoffs. Give your audience something it can answer.

1. The decision

State the exact policy or practice being decided. Name the age group, AI use, setting, purpose, and decision-maker.

2. Our recommendation

State what should be prohibited, tested, permitted with conditions, or expanded—and why.

3. What we learned

Present the strongest evidence. Distinguish facts, stakeholder testimony, interpretation, and uncertainty.

4. Who is affected

Explain likely benefits, burdens, and risks. Name whose voice remains missing.

5. Ownership and value

Who owns the data, models, infrastructure, student work, and economic value? What should remain public, local, portable, or collectively governed?

6. Rules, rights, and responsibilities

Specify permitted and prohibited uses, adult responsibilities, student rights, privacy, disclosure, access, accountability, appeal, and conditions for ending the use.

7. What happens first

Name the first action, who is responsible, the resources required, and how we will know it happened.

8. Dissent and uncertainty

Record meaningful disagreement and evidence that could change your recommendation.

9. What we are asking from our audience

Ask the receiving institution to respond by a specific date: what it accepts, rejects, or will test, and why.

10. The public response

After the presentation, record what your audience heard, what it questioned, and what it will do next.
