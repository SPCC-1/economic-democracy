The core Civic Lab

Four sessions from public question to public work.

Each 75–90 minute session moves students from first impressions toward a defensible recommendation.

Download the run of show
Session 1

The decision is already here.

Purpose: Move from abstract opinions about AI to a specific public decision.

Students: read NYC policy, distinguish types of AI use, map what is decided and what remains open, and write a first position.

Output: a one-sentence decision statement, open questions, and a stakeholder map.

Exit question: What is the most important decision NYC still has to make?

Session 2

Evidence, interests, and lived experience.

Purpose: Test assumptions against the experience of people affected.

Students: investigate learning, relationships, privacy, equity, agency, work, and public value; then conduct or analyze stakeholder interviews.

Output: supported claims, a claim weakened by evidence, and a map of benefits, risks, and incentives.

Exit question: Whose experience changes how you understand the problem?

Session 3

Ownership and governance design.

Purpose: Go beyond rules for use to ask who has power and who receives value.

Students: examine data, procurement, intellectual property, vendor dependence, accountability, and student and educator voice.

Output: a recommended position and the conditions under which it should operate.

Exit question: If the policy succeeds, who gains capability, power, and wealth?

Session 4

Public work and public response.

Purpose: Produce a recommendation an institution can answer.

Students: complete the recommendation, name meaningful disagreement, present to an authentic audience, and record the response.

Output: the Student and Community Framework, a public presentation, and a written audience response.

Exit question: What should happen next, who must act, and when should the decision be reviewed?

Want a deeper investigation?

Extend the core to eight to twelve weeks through student-led interviews, comparative policy research, approved-tool testing, community deliberation, prototype design, and publication.
