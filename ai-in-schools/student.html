Your civic challenge

Those decisions will affect you.

New York City is deciding what role artificial intelligence should play in public schools.

Students should not merely be governed by it. They should help shape it.
Download the challenge

How should New York City govern artificial intelligence in public education so that it protects human learning, prepares young people for an AI-shaped economy, and expands opportunity?

Your job is not to prove that AI is good or bad. Your job is to investigate what different forms of AI do, who benefits, who carries the risks, and what rules would serve students and the public.

What will you investigate?

  • What kinds of AI are already present in school and daily life?
  • What helps people learn, and what gets in the way?
  • What should students, families, and teachers be able to know or refuse?
  • Who owns the data, the technology, and work created with it?
  • Who makes the rules, and whose voice is missing?
  • What would fair access and meaningful preparation look like?

What will you make?

  1. Define one specific decision.
  2. Map the people and institutions involved.
  3. Gather evidence and listen to lived experience.
  4. Compare possible approaches.
  5. Recommend rules, rights, responsibilities, and safeguards.
  6. Present your work to people who can respond.

You do not need to be an AI expert.

You can complete the Civic Lab without using generative AI. Curiosity, careful listening, evidence, judgment, and collaboration matter more than technical expertise.

What counts as strong public work?

  • It makes a clear choice.
  • It uses evidence honestly.
  • It includes people who experience the issue differently.
  • It explains tradeoffs instead of hiding them.
  • It asks who has power and who receives value.
  • It offers a practical next step.
  • It names uncertainty and meaningful disagreement.
