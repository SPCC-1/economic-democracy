New York City is making a generational economic decision — to build and own grocery stores in the neighborhoods the market left behind. The decision deserves a deliberation that matches its scale.

The Proposition

The City has committed roughly $70 million to open five publicly owned grocery stores, one borough at a time. The first opens in Hunts Point in 2027. The remaining four will follow over the next several years.

That rollout is not just a procurement schedule. It is five chances to ask a community what kind of economic future it wants — and to fund a structured, public deliberation that produces a real answer the City can use.

This document proposes a civic instrument built for that purpose, paced to the City's own rollout, ready to begin in the Bronx and travel borough by borough. It does not ask the City to fund another youth program. It asks the City to fund the conversation the policy itself deserves.

What Follows

Three pages, one argument.

The proposition above is the headline. The next three pages tell the rest of it — what the City is actually deciding, the instrument we are proposing, and what a 2-to-3-year partnership would look like.

I

The question beneath the question

The City's grocery decision reads, at the surface, as where to put a store. Beneath that sits a deeper question — about the structure of public investment itself, and the choice between subsidizing a gap and helping a community own the structure that closes it. The deliberation we are proposing teaches both honestly.

II

The civic instrument

What a borough-wide public demonstration of learning actually looks like — schools, CBOs, churches, the Borough President's office, the City. The output is not a class project. It is a synthesized borough position, produced by the community, delivered to the administration.

III

The 2-to-3 year partnership

Paced to the City's own rollout — one borough-wide convening per store, beginning in the Bronx, traveling borough by borough as the stores open. Not a contract for a curriculum. A partnership to build civic-economic deliberation as infrastructure the City can keep.
