Section III · The Ask

A 2-to-3 year partnership.

Paced to the City's own rollout — one borough-wide cycle per store, beginning small in the Bronx, scaling and traveling as the model proves itself. Not a contract for a curriculum. A partnership to build civic-economic deliberation as infrastructure the City can keep.

What We Are Asking For

What we are proposing is not a one-time pilot, and it is not an open-ended commitment. It is a structured multi-year partnership — small in year one, scaled in year two, traveling in year three — that builds an instrument the City can continue running with or without us once it is established.

The pacing is set by the City itself. The Hunts Point store opens in 2027. The remaining four stores follow over the next several years. Each store opening is the natural anchor for one borough-wide cycle, with the cycle running in the months leading up to and following the opening — long enough for an informed public to do the work, short enough that the moment is alive.

The City has already committed roughly $70 million to the stores themselves. What we are asking for is a small fraction of that — invested in the civic process the policy itself deserves.

The Three-Year Arc

How the partnership scales.

Each year does specific work. Each year produces a specific deliverable. The partnership is structured so that the City sees results from year one before committing to year two, and from year two before committing to year three.

Year 1 Prove it

A first Bronx cycle

One borough · one issue · one position paper

A single first cycle in the Bronx, anchored on the Hunts Point grocery decision. One trained cohort of 12–15 teachers and program leaders. 150–300 participants across five or six sites — DYCD-funded after-school programs, public schools willing to run the lab as an elective or unit, and a small set of faith-community and CBO partners. One public demonstration of learning, hosted at the Borough President's office. One first position paper delivered to the administration.

The output: a working proof of concept, a documented model, and the Bronx's first informed public voice on the grocery decision.

Indicative Year 1 scope

Funded at roughly the scale of the small proposal already in your hands — $50,000 — covering curriculum localization, trainer cohort, facilitation of the public event, and the position paper.

Year 2 Scale it

Bronx at scale

Same borough · expanded reach · deeper cohorts

Year two takes the proven model and runs it at full borough scale — multiple parallel cohorts across the Bronx, deeper partnerships with the schools and CBOs that joined in year one, additional faith communities pulled into the train-the-trainer pool. The cohort that ran year one becomes the senior layer of trainers for year two. The model compounds.

The output: the Bronx's second position paper — broader, more representative, more rigorous — and a documented blueprint for traveling the model to the next borough.

Indicative Year 2 scope

A larger commitment, sized to the expanded scope. The specific figure is part of what we would scope together based on year-one learnings and the next store's opening timeline.

Year 3 Travel it

The next borough

Same model · new borough · new issue

Year three is where the practice becomes a citywide instrument. The model travels to the borough hosting the next store opening, with Bronx trainers helping seed the new cohort. The curriculum is localized to that borough's flagship issue. The Bronx cycle continues at lighter intensity, anchored by the cohort that ran it twice.

The output: two boroughs running cycles in parallel — and a proven, transferable model the City can continue to deploy borough by borough, issue by issue, as the rollout continues and beyond.

Indicative Year 3 scope

Sized to two parallel boroughs. The shape of the City's own contribution would shift as the model matures — earlier years are heavier on facilitation; later years are heavier on infrastructure the City increasingly owns.

What the Partnership Includes

What you would actually get.

A partnership of this kind is not just facilitation. It is the production and operation of a complete civic instrument. The City would receive — and over time, increasingly own — the following.

A localized curriculum

The Economic Democracy curriculum, with a New York City wrapper localized to the issue and the borough — language, examples, and data tuned for the people who will use it.

A trained cohort

Teachers, youth-program leaders, and community trainers prepared to teach the material honestly and run the lab with their groups — the load-bearing layer that makes the whole thing run.

A facilitated public event

The demonstration of learning, staged with the Borough President's office, with documentation, press support, and structured deliberation that produces real synthesis.

A published position paper

The borough's informed civic voice on the issue — written, public, delivered to the administration — that every commissioner, council member, and reporter can read in fifteen minutes.

A documented model

The full operating playbook — curriculum, trainer protocols, event structure, position-paper drafting process — so the City increasingly owns the instrument rather than renting it from us.

A continuing relationship

Curriculum updates as the City's policy landscape changes, a borough-by-borough cohort that compounds across the years, and a partner that knows the work because we built it with you.

What's Open

What only the City can decide.

There are real questions about how a partnership like this should be structured that only the administration can answer. We are not bringing terms. We are bringing an instrument and a starting structure. The following are the questions we would need to work through together.

  1. Where is it housed? DYCD is one natural home — the out-of-school infrastructure, the CBO contracting surface, the youth-development frame all fit. But it could also sit across DYCD and the Borough President's office, or be incubated jointly.
  2. Who else needs to be in the room? The Mayor's Office of Civic Engagement, the Department of Education, the Department of Small Business Services, the City Council — depending on the issue and the borough, different combinations make sense. Year one's first cycle could test one combination; year two adjusts.
  3. How is the City's contribution sequenced? Year one is fundable from existing DYCD discretionary authority. Year two and three may benefit from additional or different funding streams as the model becomes citywide.
  4. Who owns what, over time? The intent is that the City increasingly owns the instrument as the years compound — and that what begins as a contract becomes infrastructure. The pacing and shape of that transition is part of the conversation.
What We Are Actually Asking

A conversation, not a contract.

This document is not a proposal you are being asked to approve. It is the longer version of a conversation we would like to have with you and the people you would invite to the table. The ask is a meeting.

If what is described across these four pages is something the administration sees promise in, we would like to begin scoping a year-one Bronx cycle in earnest — together, with whatever cross-agency input you think appropriate, on a timeline that fits the Hunts Point store opening and the rest of the policy as it unfolds.

What we have on offer, in addition to everything described here, is something simpler: twenty years of teaching the population the policy is meant to serve, a body of curriculum already built and already on the public site, and a deep commitment to doing this in New York for New York.

The decision belongs to the City. The conversation around it, if you would like, can begin now.
