01The Concept Library

Standalone economic-literacy primers (~5 pages each, same template). An educator pulls one in whenever a lesson needs the underlying idea. Each teaches the tool, then breaks its neutrality across real contexts. Self-contained; no prerequisites.

Allocation

Scarcity

Limited means against unlimited wants — the starting condition of all economics.

Opportunity Cost

The real cost of any choice is the best thing given up to make it.

Incentives

People respond to rewards and penalties — often in unintended ways.

Engines

Profit Motive

The drive for gain as the engine that organizes production.

Compounding

Growth on growth — the quiet force behind both wealth and debt.

Competition

Rivalry that disciplines price and quality — until it doesn't.

Productivity New

More output per hour — the engine of living standards, and the question of who gets the gains.

Creative Destruction New

The new rises by destroying the old — growth and disruption as one motion.

Power

Monopoly

One seller, no rivals — pricing power and its costs.

Network Effects

Value that grows with users — and tips markets toward a single winner.

Economies of Scale

Cheaper per unit as you grow bigger — an advantage that compounds.

Rent-Seeking

Getting rich by capturing value rather than creating it.

Sovereignty New

Control over your own economic fate — and the leverage dependence hands to others. Person to nation.

The Trust New

A structure that splits control from benefit — protective for a child, dynastic for a fortune.

Distribution

The Commons New

Resources owned by no one and used by all — ruined without rules, governable with them.

Money & Value

Ownership & Equity

What it means to own a share of something, and why it matters.

Inflation

A general rise in prices — and the silent redistribution it performs.

Wages & Assets

Income from work versus income from what you own. (Reader-prefixed; functions as a primer.)

The Wealth of Nations

The US vs. world private-wealth-creation engine.

The Wealth of the Nation

The internal breakdown — distribution data, no moralizing.

Data New

The strange new asset — worthless alone, immense in bulk, generated by the people who rarely own it.

Cryptocurrency New

Money without a state behind it — and the question of what makes anything money at all.

The Corporation New

The legal "person" — limited liability, and the buried question of who a company is for.

P&L vs. Balance Sheet New

Flow vs. stock — "are you making money?" against "what are you worth?" Paycheck to national debt.

Credit New

Spending the future on a promise — and the trust that decides who gets to.

Debt New

A fixed, enforceable obligation — risk shifted to the borrower, power handed to the lender.
